    How to use Aurora Predictor

    This app shows where and when you are most likely to see the northern lights over the next three nights. It combines live solar wind, NOAA's aurora forecast and ECMWF's cloud forecast.

    Quick start

    1. Tap anywhere on the map, pick one of the spots along the bottom, or use 📍 My location.
    2. Read the big number: your chance of seeing the aurora right now at that spot.
    3. Check Best time each night to plan, and open the Live tab before heading out to look for clear sky.

    Map layers

    Switch with the buttons at the top.

    Viewing
    The overall chance of seeing the aurora: aurora activity × darkness × clear sky. Brighter yellow is better. This is the one to use for planning.
    Aurora
    How likely the aurora is overhead or low on the northern horizon, ignoring clouds. It is faded where it is daylight.
    Clouds
    ECMWF cloud forecast. White means cloudy. Clouds load for the area you are looking at when you zoom in (the dashed box shows what is loaded).
    Live
    What the sky is doing now: satellite cloud (white) and rain or snow radar (blue to red) for the last two hours, over the current aurora (green). Press ▶ to see which way the clouds are moving.

    The time slider

    Drag it to see the map at any hour over the next three days, or press ▶ to animate. On the Live tab it steps back through the last two hours instead.

    The forecast for a spot

    • Big number: chance of seeing the aurora right now, with aurora activity, darkness, cloud and the "feels like" temperature underneath.
    • Best time each night: the best hour of each night, with Kp, cloud and temperature.
    • Hour by hour: four rows (viewing chance, aurora, darkness, clear sky). Brighter is better. Tap or hover over a column for details.
    • On a phone, drag the sheet's handle up for more, or down to close it.

    What the labels mean:

    Excellent50% or more
    Good30–50%
    Possible15–30%
    Low5–15%
    Unlikelyunder 5%

    The solar wind bar

    The bar at the top left shows the solar wind measured right now by a spacecraft 1.5 million km towards the Sun. It reaches Earth in about 30–60 minutes. Tap it for details.

    • Bz below zero (southward) is what drives the aurora. The more negative, and the longer it stays negative, the better.
    • Kp is the overall activity level, from 0 to 9. In Iceland Kp 2–3 can already give a good show on a dark, clear night.
    • Weak / Moderate / Strong summarises how strongly the solar wind is driving the aurora.

    Tips

    • Clear sky matters most. Drive to wherever the Live and Clouds layers show a gap.
    • The best hours are usually 21:00 to 02:00. Get away from city lights and give your eyes 15 minutes to adjust.
    • Forecasts more than a day ahead are rough. Check again on the night, and keep an eye on Bz.
    • Faint aurora often looks grey-green to the eye. A phone camera on night mode shows it much more clearly.

    Put it on your phone

    • iPhone (Safari): Share → Add to Home Screen.
    • Android (Chrome): ⋮ menu → Install app.

    It then opens full-screen like an app, and still shows the last forecast it loaded if you lose signal.

    Where the data comes from

    Solar wind, aurora nowcast and Kp forecast: NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center. Weather: ECMWF via Open-Meteo. Satellite: EUMETSAT Meteosat. Radar: RainViewer. All of it is free and open. If something won't load, tap the solar wind bar to see the status of each data source.

    This is a guide, not a guarantee. The aurora can appear when it's forecast to be quiet, and stay away when it's forecast to be active.
